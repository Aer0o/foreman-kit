# Proofread before sending

The second pair of eyes. Find what would embarrass him or cost him money, report it worst first, and don't
touch numbers without asking.

## Read, in one go

One read through the matching skill (docx, pdf, xlsx or pptx) that pulls everything: body, headers,
footers, tables, comments, speaker notes, hidden rows and sheets, file properties (title, author,
company). Plus `~/.claude/foreman/profile.md` (his company name, address, phone, licence and HST numbers).
Pasted text: check it as it stands.

## Check

1. **Money:** line items add up to subtotals and totals; quantity x rate = line amount; tax on the right
   subtotal at the stated rate (Ontario HST is 13%); the same figure everywhere (summary, table, cover
   email); amounts in words match figures. All arithmetic in one calculation, never by eye.
2. **Units** present and consistent (A, V, kVA, kW, AWG, mm, ft or m, hours); no metric and imperial mixed
   for one thing without reason; no slip that changes meaning (V where every other line says A).
3. **Dates:** weekdays match dates (calculate). Start before finish. Quote validity not expired. Right year,
   especially in January. One date format.
4. **Names:** client, company, site address, people, job or quote numbers spelled the same everywhere; his
   details match profile.md.
5. **Leftovers:** `[NEEDS: ...]`, XXX, TBD, TBC, ???, lorem ipsum, "insert here", yellow highlights, empty
   cells meant to be filled, comments, unaccepted tracked changes.
6. **Old-template traces:** another client's name, address, job number or project anywhere, including
   header, footer, file name and properties. A name that appears once or twice while the rest says another
   is the usual sign.
7. **Attachments and references:** "see attached", "enclosed", "drawing E-2", "Appendix B", "photos below"
   each exist, in the document or the folder he's sending from. Can't tell: ask.
8. **Spelling:** Canadian (colour, centre, metre, labour, cheque, licence noun, license verb; -ize is fine).
   Product and company names keep their spelling. Typos, doubled words, wrong words (their/there,
   affect/effect, complement/compliment).
9. **Tone:** one clear ask, no apologising for the price, no ALL CAPS or sarcasm, no filler or AI phrasing
   ("I hope this email finds you well", "please don't hesitate", "seamless"). Point it out; don't rewrite.

## Report back

Worst first, up to three groups (skip empty ones):

- **Fix before sending:** wrong client name, a total that doesn't add up, missing attachment, leftover
  placeholder, wrong date or weekday, expired validity.
- **Should fix:** mismatched names or units, spelling, tone.
- **Small stuff:** one line of formatting nits, unless he asks for detail.

One line each, saying where and what: "Page 2: the items add up to a different amount than the total line.
Which one is right?" Nothing found: say what you checked in one line. Don't invent problems.

## Fixing

Numbers and money: never fix silently; show both values and ask. Everything else: ask once, "Want me to
fix the rest?" If yes, all fixes in one script on a v2 copy, then list what changed. Pasted text: give back
the corrected version.
