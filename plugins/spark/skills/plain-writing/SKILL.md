# Plain writing

Strip the patterns that make text read as machine-written or padded, so his reports, emails and proposals
read like a capable tradesperson wrote them.

Adapted from stop-slop by Hardik Pandya (MIT licence, see [LICENSE](LICENSE)).

**Never change** numbers, prices, quantities, dates, names, addresses, model and part numbers, code
references, his technical terms ("sub-panel" stays "sub-panel"), phrases `~/.claude/spark/voice.md` says he
uses, or the meaning of anything he wrote. Never add a detail to sound more concrete.

## Rules

1. **Lead with the point.** First sentence says what happened, what you found, or what you need.
2. **Cut filler:** throat-clearing openers, emphasis crutches, filler adverbs, stock email padding. Keep
   adverbs that change the meaning ("fails intermittently"). See [references/phrases.md](references/phrases.md).
3. **Break formulaic structures:** "Not X, but Y", lists of what something isn't, dramatic fragments,
   rhetorical questions. See [references/structures.md](references/structures.md).
4. **Name who does what.** "We replaced the breaker", "the GC's crew removed the ceiling". Passive only when
   the actor is unknown or doesn't matter ("the panel was installed around 1980"). "The inspection
   revealed" becomes "we found".
5. **Be specific with what you have:** the panel, the room, the date, the quantity from his notes. Cut
   vague claims ("significant issues were identified") or replace them with the issue.
6. **Say it once.** No summary repeated at the end of every section; don't interpret every photo.
7. **Trust the reader.** No softening or over-explaining. An owner or GC knows their job.
8. **Vary the rhythm.** Mix sentence lengths; don't default to lists of three; one or two dashes per
   document at most.
9. **Prune AI vocabulary:** delve, underscore, showcase, pivotal, intricate, meticulous, realm, align,
   underpin, garner, bolster, notably, crucial, comprehensive, robust, nuanced, leverage, foster, seamless,
   tapestry, landscape, testament, navigate (meaning "handle"), boast, elevate, streamline, ensure (when
   "make sure" or nothing will do).
10. **Don't swap one pattern for another.** Not every paragraph should end on a short punchy line.

## By kind of writing

- **Reports:** headings, tables and numbered lists are right here. Findings flat; recommendations as
  instructions ("Replace the two scorched breakers").
- **Emails:** short, one ask, no warm-up or wind-down. Sign-off from voice.md.
- **Proposals and cover letters:** confident and concrete. Cut sales talk ("industry-leading", "one-stop
  shop", "peace of mind", "we pride ourselves on", "commitment to excellence"); use what he actually does
  (licensed, years in business, kind of jobs, warranty) if he gave you those facts.

## Quick checks

Opening gets to the point? Any "I hope this finds you well", "please be advised", "at your earliest
convenience"? Any "not X, it's Y" (state Y)? A thing doing a person's job ("the data shows")? A vague claim
with nothing behind it? The same point twice? Three same-length sentences in a row?

Before and after examples: [references/examples.md](references/examples.md).

## Hand it back

Pasted text: the revised text. A file: a v2 copy beside the original, all changes in one pass. Add one line
on the kind of changes ("Cut the padding, put the ask first"); no lecture, no score. Same length or
shorter. If it was already plain, say so and change little.
