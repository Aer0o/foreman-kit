# Plain writing

Strip the patterns that make text read as machine-written or padded, so his reports, emails and proposals
read like a capable tradesperson wrote them.

Adapted from stop-slop by Hardik Pandya (MIT licence, see [LICENSE](LICENSE)).

## Never change

- Numbers, prices, quantities, dates, names, addresses, model and part numbers, code references.
- Technical terms he used. "Sub-panel" stays "sub-panel".
- Phrases `~/.claude/spark/voice.md` says he uses, and the meaning of anything he wrote.
- Never add a fact, figure or detail to make the text sound more concrete. Specifics come from his
  material or not at all.

## Rules

1. **Lead with the point.** First sentence says what happened, what you found, or what you need.
2. **Cut filler.** Throat-clearing openers, emphasis crutches, filler adverbs, stock email padding. Keep
   adverbs that change the meaning ("fails intermittently"). See [references/phrases.md](references/phrases.md).
3. **Break formulaic structures.** "Not X, but Y", lists of what something isn't, dramatic fragments,
   rhetorical questions. See [references/structures.md](references/structures.md).
4. **Name who does what.** "We replaced the breaker", "the GC's crew removed the ceiling". Passive is fine
   when the actor is unknown or doesn't matter ("the panel was installed around 1980"). Things don't act
   on their own: "the inspection revealed" becomes "we found".
5. **Be specific with what you have.** Name the actual thing from his notes: the panel, the room, the date,
   the quantity. Cut vague claims ("significant issues were identified") or replace them with the issue.
6. **Say it once.** State the main point where it matters most. Don't repeat the summary at the end of every
   section or interpret every photo.
7. **Trust the reader.** No softening, no over-explaining, no hand-holding. An owner or GC knows their job.
8. **Vary the rhythm.** Mix sentence lengths. Don't default to lists of three. Go easy on dashes; one or
   two in a document is plenty.
9. **Prune AI vocabulary:** delve, underscore, showcase, pivotal, intricate, meticulous, realm, align,
   underpin, garner, bolster, notably, crucial, comprehensive, robust, nuanced, leverage, foster, seamless,
   tapestry, landscape, testament, navigate (meaning "handle"), boast, elevate, streamline, ensure (when
   "make sure" or nothing will do).
10. **Don't swap one pattern for another.** De-padding can create a new sameness: every paragraph ending on
    a short punchy line, a dash in every sentence. Let some paragraphs stay plain and even.

## By kind of writing

- **Reports:** headings, tables and numbered lists are good here; a tidy structure is right for a report.
  Findings stated flatly. Recommendations as instructions ("Replace the two scorched breakers").
- **Emails:** short, one ask, no warm-up and no wind-down. Sign-off from voice.md.
- **Proposals and cover letters:** confident and concrete. Cut sales talk: "industry-leading",
  "state-of-the-art", "one-stop shop", "peace of mind", "we pride ourselves on", "unparalleled",
  "commitment to excellence". Replace with what he actually does: licensed, how long in business, the
  kind of jobs, the warranty, if he gave you those facts.

## Quick checks

- Opening line gets to the point?
- Any "I hope this finds you well", "please be advised", "at your earliest convenience"? Cut.
- Any "not X, it's Y" or "it's not just X, it's Y"? State Y.
- Any sentence where a thing does a person's job ("the data shows", "the report highlights")? Name the person.
- Any vague claim with no specific behind it? Name the thing or cut it.
- Same point made twice? Keep the stronger one.
- Three sentences in a row the same length? Break one.
- Every paragraph ending on a punchline? Vary them.
- Any AI vocabulary from rule 9?

Before and after examples for his kind of work: [references/examples.md](references/examples.md).

## How to hand it back

- **He asked directly** ("tighten this up"): give back the revised text, or for a file a v2 copy following
  the **doc-edit** skill. Add one line on what kind of changes you made ("Cut the padding, put the ask
  first"). No lecture, no score.
- **Another skill called this as its final pass:** apply it quietly and carry on.
- Keep the length the same or shorter. If the original was already plain, say so and change little.
