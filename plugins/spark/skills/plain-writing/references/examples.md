# Before and after

Adapted from stop-slop by Hardik Pandya (MIT licence, see ../LICENSE). The details in these examples are
made up for illustration; in real work every detail comes from his material.

## 1. Email padding

**Before:**
> I hope this email finds you well. I just wanted to reach out and follow up regarding the outstanding
> invoice for the work we completed at your property. Please do not hesitate to contact me if you have any
> questions. Thank you for your patience and understanding.

**After:**
> Invoice [number] for the panel upgrade at [site] was due on [date] and is still open. Can you let me know
> by Friday when it will be paid? A copy is attached.

**Changes:** cut the warm-up and the wind-down, named the invoice, made one ask with a date.

## 2. Vague report finding

**Before:**
> During the course of the inspection, several significant issues were identified that may potentially
> impact the safety and reliability of the electrical system going forward.

**After:**
> We found three problems that need fixing before the unit is occupied: scorched insulation on the
> 40 A breaker feeding the range, a missing bond on the water line, and no GFCI protection on the bathroom
> receptacle.

**Changes:** named the problems (from his notes), named who found them, said when they matter.

## 3. Proposal sales talk

**Before:**
> We pride ourselves on delivering industry-leading, comprehensive electrical solutions with an
> unwavering commitment to excellence, ensuring a seamless experience from start to finish.

**After:**
> We're a licensed electrical contractor doing commercial fit-outs. We'll run the job with one site lead,
> and our workmanship carries a one-year warranty.

**Changes:** replaced claims with facts he gave you. If he hasn't given the facts, cut the sentence.

## 4. Binary contrast and false agency

**Before:**
> The delay isn't about our crew. It's about the drywall schedule. The timeline has shifted and the
> completion date now looks different.

**After:**
> The drywall crew finished on the 12th instead of the 5th, so we can't start the device install until
> next week. New completion date: [date].

**Changes:** dropped the "isn't X, it's Y" pivot, said who did what, gave the new date.

## 5. Restating the point

**Before:**
> In summary, as outlined above, the main panel is at the end of its service life. Replacement is
> recommended. To reiterate, we strongly recommend replacing the main panel.

**After:**
> We recommend replacing the main panel.

**Changes:** said it once.
