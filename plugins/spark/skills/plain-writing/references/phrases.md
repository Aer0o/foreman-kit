# Phrases to remove

Adapted from stop-slop by Hardik Pandya (MIT licence, see ../LICENSE).

## Throat-clearing openers

Announcements before the point. Cut them and state the point.

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what / why / how [X]"
- "The truth is," / "The reality is"
- "It turns out"
- "The real [X] is"
- "Let me be clear"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "I wanted to reach out" / "I'm reaching out to"
- "I'm writing to let you know that"
- "Just a quick note to"

## Stock email and letter padding

Common in business mail and empty every time. Cut, or replace with the plain version.

| Cut | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "I hope this email finds you well" | (nothing; start with the point) |
| "Please be advised that" / "Kindly note that" | (nothing; say the thing) |
| "Please do not hesitate to contact me" | "Call me if you have questions." or nothing |
| "At your earliest convenience" | a date: "by Thursday" |
| "Thank you for your patience" | (nothing, or thank them for something specific) |
| "As per our conversation" | "As we discussed on the phone Tuesday" |
| "Just following up" / "Just checking in" | say what you need: "Can you confirm the start date?" |
| "We are pleased to submit" | "Here is our quote for" |
| "Going forward" / "Moving forward" | "From now on", "next", or nothing |
| "Rest assured" | (nothing; state what you'll do) |

## Emphasis crutches

Add nothing. Delete.

- "Full stop." / "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "Make no mistake"
- "This matters because" / "Here's why that matters"
- "It's important to note that" / "It's worth noting"

## Business jargon

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Navigate (challenges) | handle, deal with |
| Leverage | use |
| Utilize | use |
| Facilitate | help, run, arrange |
| Touch base / circle back | call, talk, get back to you |
| Deep dive | look closely at, review |
| On the same page | agreed |
| Bandwidth | time, crew available |
| Game-changer | say what it changes |
| Best-in-class / industry-leading / state-of-the-art | say what's actually good about it |

## Filler adverbs

Cut the ones that add emphasis but no meaning. Keep adverbs that change the meaning ("trips
intermittently", "temporarily energized", "intentionally left blank"). Usual offenders:

really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually, deeply, truly, fundamentally, inherently,
inevitably, interestingly, importantly, crucially, extremely, very.

Filler phrases:

- "At its core"
- "In today's [X]"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "In order to" (use "to")
- "Due to the fact that" (use "because")

## Meta-commentary

The document should move, not describe its own structure.

- "In this report, we will..."
- "As we'll see..."
- "Let me walk you through..."
- "The following section outlines..."
- "It should be noted that..."

## Vague declaratives

Sentences that announce importance without naming the specific thing. Cut, or replace with the thing.

- "Several significant issues were identified."
- "The implications are significant."
- "The stakes are high."
- "This presents a number of challenges."

"Several significant issues were identified" becomes the issues: "We found two overheated connections in
the main panel and an ungrounded receptacle circuit in the kitchen" (only if his notes say so).
